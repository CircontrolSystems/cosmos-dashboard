KPI calculation — Cosmos Dashboard
All KPIs are calculated over the time range shown in the top badge (minimum–maximum of the loaded data), unless a manual date filter is applied.
MTTR — Mean Time To Repair
MTTR = Σ alarm_duration / no. of alarms with duration > 0
Average resolution time per alarm. Alarms with no recorded duration (0 or null) are not counted in the denominator.
MTBF — Mean Time Between Failures
MTBF = observation_span_h / (no_equipment_failures / no_chargers)
Average time between failures per charger. The observation span is the time in hours between the first and last alarm/session in the dataset. The higher the MTBF, the more reliable the network.
Only equipment failures count: Charging Session Attempt alarms (session start failure, measured with the CSR) and Websocket Closed/Unstable alarms (network connectivity, with its own Uptime · Connection KPI) are excluded from the count. The Errors/Alarms counter and the MTTR do include them.
Global uptime — Estimated availability
Uptime = (1 − Σdowntime_union_h / (span_h × no_chargers)) × 100
For each charger, downtime intervals are calculated as a union (not a simple sum) within the observation window. In multi-company mode, each charger uses its own individual span to avoid distortion.
Incid./100 — Incidents per 100 units
Incid./100 = round(no_alarms / no_chargers × 100)
Normalised rate for comparing fleets of different sizes. A value of 120 → each charger generated 1.2 alarms on average in the period.
Uptime per connector (Fleet view)
Uptime_conn = (1 − downtime_conn_h / span_h) × 100
Calculated independently for each connector. Connectors with no alarms show 100% uptime (they appear in the table thanks to CDR sessions, not only alarms).
MTBF per connector (Fleet view)
If >1 alarm: (last − first) / no_alarms · If =1: total_span
With more than one alarm, MTBF uses the connector's own range. With a single alarm, MTBF is assumed to equal the total span of the dataset.
Critical charger ("Critical only" checkbox)
Critical if: downtime/span > 10% OR errors/day > 3
A charger is critical if its downtime exceeds 10% of the analysed period, or if it generates more than 3 alarms per day on average. Either condition sets the flag.

Welcome to Cosmos Cloud
Operations & Maintenance Dashboard
This tool turns the raw data from your charger network (Chargers, Alarms and Sessions) into actionable KPIs — availability, MTBF, MTTR, incidents, energy delivered and usage patterns — so you can identify critical chargers, recurring errors and opportunities for improvement at each company and site.
Network overview
Fleet status, uptime, alarms, energy and billing — filterable by company, site and date.
Critical charger detection
Cross-references alarms and sessions to highlight units with high downtime or recurring errors.
Usage & energy analysis
Session volume, kWh delivered, revenue and hourly patterns to optimise operations.

Connect to the network to load the data
The Cosmos Cloud server will give you the list of available companies. Select one (or all) and the dashboard will fill in automatically.

What's new in the Dashboard Updated on 6 November 2026
v.06.11.2026 CURRENT Current version · released on 6 November 2026
● MTBF, incidents and repair time count failures, not alarms
A failure starts when a connector goes from available to faulted. Alarms received while the connector is already faulted (for example after a TriggerMessage), alarms that flap within 5 minutes of recovering and the connector-0 «all connectors» alarm no longer count as new failures. A connector is considered available again after a closed alarm, a charging session or a charging session attempt on it. Repair time is measured per failure. Error detail shows Failures (what reduces MTBF) next to Alarms (everything received).
● Faults in progress now count as unavailability
An alarm that is still open (no duration) counts as unavailability up to the last data point only if, after it, the charger has no closed equipment alarm and no charging session: the charger is most likely still faulted. Old open alarms followed by closed alarms or sessions are ignored (alarms the platform did not close). These chargers appear in Chargers to watch as «Fault in progress» and in Fleet view as «Open fault». Silent chargers now also include chargers with no session in the whole window that had charged before.
● Advanced view reorganised
The Chargers and Overview tabs are removed (Errors without code stays for now, to follow that case charger by charger). Clicking a charger now opens Charger detail filtered by that charger. The Summary adds Top errors by impact (with a link to Error detail) and Firmware distribution by model.
● Fleet view: severity levels
Opens on the last 7 days of data with Critical chargers only; switch to Critical + Warning or All. Each card shows why it is on the list (open fault, low availability, outage over 24 h, low CSR, no sessions) and cards are sorted from most to least severe. Quick ranges (Today, 7d, 30d) now count back from the last date with data.
● Charger detail: Compare by
The second view is now Compare: one row per model, firmware, site or model × firmware with availability, MTBF, CSR, incidents and unavailability hours per charger and sessions per charger per day, using the same calculation as the Summary.
● Error detail: better diagnosis
New firmware filter. Frequency and duration can be shown as two bar charts or as a bubble chart (frequency vs duration, size = chargers). The master table becomes the Error impact table: incidents and hours with their share, chargers, top-3 concentration (unit vs systemic problem), median duration, trend vs the previous period, main firmware and model. New pattern detection: frequent sequences of errors within 60 s on the same charger (with an example second by second) and recurrence of the same code within 7 days.
● Charging session attempt: user impact
Failed attempts are split into left without charging and retried and charged (a session on the same charger within 10 min), with CSR by connector type, failed attempts by hour of day and attempt duration. Alarm times (UTC) are converted to local time to match sessions.
● The dashboard is now in English
All views, charts, tables, tooltips, exports and messages are in English, with English number and date formats.
● Basic view: one screen, reliability first
The Basic view is now a single screen. It opens on the last complete month and starts with the four Reliability KPIs; click any of them (Availability, Network MTBF, CSR, Connection drops) to see it month by month in the chart below, and click a month in the chart to select it. Then comes Fleet with the current charger status and a short list of chargers to watch (lowest availability or no recent sessions; by default over the last 30 days of data, with a selector for the last 7 days or the selected period), and finally Charging activity, now with usage per charger (sessions per day), and its charts. «OCPP disconnections» is now called Connection drops.
● New Summary for the Basic view
The automatic findings are gone and all KPIs now have the same weight, grouped by category: Fleet (chargers in service, firmware compliance, silent chargers), Reliability (availability, network MTBF, charging session start CSR, OCPP disconnections) and Charging activity (sessions, total energy, average energy and time per session). Current charger status moves up, followed by availability by month, network usage, when charging happens and energy per charger per day.
● Advanced view keeps the detail
In the Advanced view the Summary also shows the automatic findings, repair time, main cause by hours, diagnostic coverage, average power vs rated, no-energy or short sessions, causes of unavailability, monthly trend, silent chargers and average power by model.
v.05.11.2026 PREVIOUS Released on 5 November 2026
● The dashboard waits until all companies are loaded
With "All companies", the dashboard is not shown until all of them have been downloaded. Each company is tried up to 3 times and the Excel files have up to 2 minutes to download (previously 8 s, and with a slow VPN companies were silently dropped). If any still fail, you are told which ones and can Retry or Continue without them; in that case a "N companies missing" warning appears in the top bar so you can try again. Companies are downloaded 3 at a time and kept in memory: switching between one company and "All" no longer downloads anything again. Also fixed a second full load that was triggered when the first one finished.
● New "Errors without code" tab (Advanced view)
Analyses OtherError alarms that arrive without a vendor code. They almost always arrive right after another error on the same charger and take its place: the connector is still faulted, but Cosmos no longer says why. The tab shows how many there are, how many hours of unavailability they cover, which pattern they fall into (masks the connector error, propagated from another connector, after CO.00.000 or no previous error) and the sequence chart for each charger: second by second and connector by connector, which error arrives and which one is masked. For the selected charger, it suggests the OCPP log windows to request. It can also be reached from Error detail, from the "OtherError without code" cause in the Summary and from the charger detail card.
● Error detail: filter by source
New Source buttons: Charger (errors sent by the charger itself over OCPP, usually with a vendor code or OtherError), Session attempts (BE.34.111) and Websocket / platform (OCPP and MQTT websocket, unstable, mass disconnections). By default only charger errors are shown; each button shows how many alarms it has and the choice is remembered. It affects the tab's KPIs, charts, tables and vendor code drop-down.
● Fixed Circutor look
The Classic / Modern UX switch has been removed: the dashboard always uses the CDS Circutor interface (side menu). Dark mode is kept.
v.03.11.2026 PREVIOUS Released on 3 November 2026
● New "Summary" start tab
Brings the old Basic view and the global indicators together on a single page: period (all or one month, compared with the previous month), automatic conclusions, availability (equipment / connection), OCPP disconnections, CSR and repair, plus two rows of global indicators. This is the tab that will open in Cosmos for all companies. Basic shows only this tab; Advanced shows all of them.
● Basic view for all companies: Summary + Chargers
The Basic view now has two tabs. Summary adds the Network usage section: energy per day or week, when charging happens (hour × day), current charger status and energy per charger and day. Chargers is a sortable, searchable list (availability, hours of unavailability, incidents, CSR, sessions, energy, last session); clicking a charger opens its detail card with its KPIs, sessions and hours of unavailability per day, causes, latest alarms explained and latest sessions. Both tabs share the period selector. The Advanced view adds the remaining tabs.
● Availability calculation fixed across the dashboard
Each charger counts from its commissioning date, overlapping unavailability periods on the same charger are counted once, and those spanning two months are split between them. Connection is only measured from when websocket data is available (before that, "—" is shown). Used by Summary, By company, By model and Charger detail.
● New Cosmos codes
The following are now classified: BO.34.110 (OCPP websocket closed), BO.37.101 / BO.37.102 (MQTT / MQTT b2b), BO.34.112 / BO.37.103 (OCPP / MQTT mass disconnection) and BE.34.111 (session attempt). Only OCPP websocket closed reduces availability; the rest is recorded under connectivity or in the CSR. The old names ("Websocket Closed", "Charging Session Attempt") are still recognised. "Websocket Unstable" no longer reduces availability.
● New KPIs
Repair: median, P90, % self-recovered in under 2 h and faults lasting more than 24 h (replaces the average MTTR). Diagnostic coverage: hours of unavailability with a useful vendor code. Silent chargers: no sessions in their last 7 days. Average vs rated power by model. Zero-energy or short sessions (formerly "Failed sessions", to avoid confusion with the CSR). Disconnections: % recovered in under 5 min.
● Hours of unavailability without double counting
Hours per cause (Unavailability causes, Error detail, Master table) are calculated as a union per charger: an alarm that repeats or occurs on several connectors counts once. "Most frequent / most critical error" becomes the main cause by hours.
● Changes to the views
By company is now a tab (only with "All companies"); "Current range / legacy range" is now the grouping in "By model". Overview: connector types grouped, charger status in a stacked bar, energy per charger and day in horizontal bars; Top sites and Top customers are hidden when they add nothing. Error detail: frequency and duration in two charts; errors by firmware cross-referenced with the model; master table by code, sorted by hours. CSR: only from when failed attempts are recorded, with weekly trend. Revenue and SoC are hidden when there is no data.
● Removed
"Global indicators" side panel, Map tab, "All alarms", Alarms by site, Top errors by vendor code, Sessions by hour, KPI summary by model in Charger detail, Error types, Total alarms and Incid./100.
● CDS Circutor look
NettoPro typeface, colours and components from the Croma design system, revised dark mode and icons instead of emojis.
v.11.06.2026.10:00 PREVIOUS Released on 11 June 2026
New SoC KPIs — quality of the charging experience
Under Global indicators → Energy & usage, the "Full charges" card shows the percentage of charging sessions that end with a final SoC ≥ 90 %. Charger detail adds the Average SoC and Charges ≥ 90 % cards, a new Final SoC of charging sessions chart (switchable between Distribution in 10 % bands and Monthly trend of average SoC) and a Session log with a final SoC column. Everything is recalculated with the site, model, charger and date filters.
ℹ How SoC is counted
Final SoC is only reported by some DC chargers. Sessions with no SoC data ("–" in the Excel: AC chargers, or DC chargers that do not send it) are excluded from all calculations so as not to distort the averages; a SoC of 0 % does count as valid data. Each chart shows the coverage (how many sessions in scope report SoC).
Firmware filter in Charger detail
New Firmware selector between Model and Charger. The available versions adjust to the selected site and model, and choosing one filters the whole tab: chargers, errors, Uptime/MTTR/MTBF, SoC KPIs, charts and both logs. Useful for comparing fleet behaviour before and after a SW update. Firmware is linked through the charger record (SW Version column).
Fix when reading session Excel files
Some exported files declare a narrower internal range than the actual cells, leaving the last columns (SoC and Tag type) out of the read. The dashboard now recalculates the actual range of the file before processing it, both when loading from the server and when loading manually.
v.03.06.2026.10:00 PREVIOUS Released on 3 June 2026
↑ More realistic uptime — inflated denominator fixed
Downtime can only be calculated from alarms, but the observation window was built from the combined range of alarms + sessions. When the session history started months before the alarm history, that earlier stretch (where no alarm was possible) was counted as 100 % available and inflated the uptime. In a real case, the denominator included ~51 % of time with no alarm data, artificially raising uptime from ~94 % to ~97 %. The calculation is now limited to the period for which alarm data exists.
▤ Data cut-off: only from 01/01/2026
A global time cut-off is applied: all records before 1 January 2026 are ignored in the three Excel files (alarms and sessions), even if the file contains earlier history. This aligns the calculation window across sources and removes the uptime distortion described above. The cut-off applies equally to uptime, MTTR, MTBF, alarms, energy and billing.
ℹ Note
The uptime shown is still an upper bound: completely silent outages (a charger that disconnects and stops transmitting) do not generate alarms with a duration, so they are not visible. Actual availability will always be equal to or lower than the value shown.

v.25.05.2026.10:00 PREVIOUS Released on 25 May 2026
— Goodbye to the false 100 % uptime
Until now, a charger (or a model, or a company) that reported no alarms and no sessions in a period was counted as having 100 % uptime, which distorted the summaries. From this version on, those cells show a — with an explanatory tooltip ("No data reported in this period — uptime cannot be calculated"). The dash also applies to MTTR, MTBF and Alarms for the same group when there is no data at all, and carries through to the ACC column, the per-connector breakdowns in Charger detail and the exported Excel files.
ℹ Why it matters
A charger that is installed but has no telemetry is not the same as a charger with 0 failures: the first has no data, the second does and it is good. Mixing them artificially inflated the overall fleet availability. With this change, the KPIs reflect only what is measured, and chargers that do not report are clearly identified as such without skewing the average.
